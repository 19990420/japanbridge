The Founder

Why ARKLinks exists

Travelling through Japan's countryside, I kept meeting extraordinary people — above all, a tea farmer whose family has worked the same hillside since the Edo period, making tea of a depth the world almost never gets to taste. The travellers who found them were moved to tears — yet the world has almost no way to reach them.

At the same time, these makers — the very foundation of Japanese culture — are quietly disappearing. Successors are scarce, and centuries of craft risk ending in a single generation. I founded ARKLinks on one conviction: that a real relationship between you and the family who grows your tea can carry this heritage forward — not through charity, but through every tin you order.

My work is simple: I walk the fields, translate the stories, and carry their tea to your door.

Tomoya Iwamae — Founder
FAQ

Frequently asked questions

When can I buy the tea?

+

The first harvest opens this autumn, in a small first run. Nothing is charged today and there are no pre-orders — the founding list is simply first in line when the shop opens.

Where do you ship?

+

We open with the United States first, with duties included in the price — no surprises at customs, delivered to your door. Further countries follow as we grow.

What will you offer?

+

A deliberately short list: single-origin sencha as the flagship, matcha from the same few farms as the doorway, and a handful of teas in between. Never more than a few farms — depth over breadth.

How is this different from tea I can already buy?

+

Every tin can be traced to a single named field. It arrives with the story of who grew it, how their family has farmed, and when it was picked — a closeness to the maker that we believe you can taste.

The water where I live is hard. Will it still taste right?

+

Yes — with a small adjustment. Japanese tea assumes soft water, so every order ships with Brewing Made Simple: a short guide to soft or filtered water and lower temperatures that lets the sweetness through, wherever you live.

Could we ever visit the farm?

+

In time, yes. We are quietly preparing private visits for the people who drink our tea — so that one day, the hillside on your label is a place you have stood. Founding members will hear about it first.

One day, we'll take you to the hillside itself.

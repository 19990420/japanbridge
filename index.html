The Fields

Two people. More than ten fields.

A single family farm in the Shizuoka mountains. A husband and wife, and more than ten separate fields between them.

Tea here grows on the sides of mountains. The flat ground at the bottom of the valley is the easy part; the higher you go, the less easy it gets. On the steepest slopes no machine can climb, so every row has to be cut by hand. And across these hills the growers are getting older — there are fewer hands each year, and the steepest ground is the first to go uncut.

More than ten fields means around thirty different teas on the shelf — and no two people are looking for the same one.

Tell us what you like.

Some people want aroma above all else. Some want the deep savoury sweetness the Japanese call umami. Some want a cup with a little astringency to it, to drink after a meal. Tell us which — and what you tend to eat and drink alongside your tea — and we will go to the farm and pick the three that suit you.

The Tea

Three teas. One of them is your call.

The kinds are settled: a black tea, a roasted tea, and a sencha. Which tea actually goes into each bag is not. The farm grows around thirty of them, and we choose from its shelf together with the grower — for the sencha, in whichever direction you point us.

01 Black tea 30 g
  • Chosen at the farm

Japan makes black tea too, from the same plant the sencha comes from. Almost nobody outside Japan has had one. This farm makes several, and which of them you get depends on how the year went.

02 Roasted 100 g
  • Chosen at the farm

Ours is made from first-flush leaf — the same leaf that goes into the sencha, roasted instead. Nothing else goes in, so the taste has to carry it on its own.

03 Sencha 100 g · you choose
  • Savory and full
  • Bright and grassy
  • Let us choose

This is the one you steer. Tell us which way you lean and we pick the field to match. Leave it to us and we send whichever is drinking best the week your order comes in — and tell you why when it ships.

We ask you the questions you can answer, and we don't ask you the ones you can't. You know what you like the taste of. Which field came in best this year is ours to work out.

Since we are the ones choosing, please don't be polite about it. Strong and grassy, or soft and sweet? Morning, or late at night? What do you eat, and what do you usually drink alongside your tea? Is there a tea you had once and have been looking for ever since? Nothing is too specific. There is a box for it at checkout, we read it before anything is packed, and the more you write the less we have to guess.

Made to order. Nothing is sitting in a warehouse — which also means that if a field is between harvests, we will choose the closest thing and tell you why when it ships.

The Founder

Why ARKLinks exists

Travelling through Japan's countryside, I kept meeting extraordinary people — above all, a tea farmer whose family has worked the same hillside since the Edo period, making tea of a depth the world almost never gets to taste. The travellers who found them were moved to tears — yet the world has almost no way to reach them.

At the same time, these makers — the very foundation of Japanese culture — are quietly disappearing. Successors are scarce, and centuries of craft risk ending in a single generation. I founded ARKLinks on one conviction: that a real relationship between you and the family who grows your tea can carry this heritage forward — not through charity, but through every box you order.

My work is simple: I walk the fields, translate the stories, and carry their tea to your door.

Tomoya Iwamae — Founder

The water where I live is hard. Will it still taste right?

+

Yes — with a small adjustment. Japanese tea assumes soft water, so hard water flattens it. Our brewing page explains what to use, how to get the temperature right without a thermometer, and gives the grams, temperature and seconds for every kind of tea we send.

Meet the Hands
That Make Japan.
Sake. Wine. Tea. Rice.

ARKLinks opens doors that stay closed to tourists — centuries-old sake breweries, mountain wineries, family tea farms and heritage rice growers. One private day, designed entirely around what moves you.

These are not tours. They are introductions — to the people who have carried Japan's craft for generations, in places where every guest arrives as a friend of the house.

Our Philosophy

Japan's finest things are not in shops.
They are in the hands of the people who make them.

Behind every remarkable bottle of sake, every bowl of matcha, every grain of heirloom rice, there is a family — often one that has refined a single craft for centuries. We believe the deepest way to know Japan is to sit with these makers, walk their fields, and taste their work where it is born. That is what we arrange.

The Experiences

Choose what moves you. We open the door.

Every journey is private and built from scratch. Combine one, two or three visits into a single day or a multi-day itinerary — always with an expert bilingual guide at your side.

Sake Brewery

Inside a Working Sake Brewery

Step into a brewery where sake has been made the same way for generations. Walk the koji room with the brewmaster, taste unreleased sake drawn straight from the tank, and understand why the world's finest restaurants are turning to Japanese sake.

Half or full day Private visit
Winery · Yamanashi

Japanese Wine at Its Source

In the vineyards beneath Mount Fuji's neighbouring peaks, meet the growers behind Japan's quietly celebrated Koshu wines. Walk the rows with the winemaker, taste through the cellar, and discover a wine culture most of the world has never touched.

Half or full day Private visit
Tea & Matcha · Shizuoka

A Family Tea Farm, Centuries Old

In the hills of Shizuoka, a family has grown tea since the Edo period. Pick leaves in their fields, learn how true matcha and sencha are made, and taste a side-by-side pairing guided by the farmer — an experience no tea shop can offer.

Half or full day Private visit
Rice · Niigata

The Rice Behind It All

Rice is the soul of Japanese food culture — and of sake itself. Visit heritage growers in Niigata's snow country, walk the paddies, and taste rice the way Japanese connoisseurs do: freshly milled, freshly cooked, unforgettable.

Founder's Story

Why I started ARKLinks

Travelling through Japan's countryside, I kept meeting extraordinary people: a tea farmer whose family has worked the same hillside since the Edo period, brewers and winemakers producing world-class craft in near anonymity. Visitors from around the world who found them were moved to tears — yet the world has almost no way to reach them.

At the same time, these producers — the very foundation of Japanese culture — are quietly disappearing. Successors are scarce, and centuries of craft risk ending in a single generation. I founded ARKLinks with one conviction: that connecting the world's most curious travellers directly with Japan's finest makers can preserve this heritage — not through charity, but through genuine encounter and lasting relationships.

My work is simple: I open the doors, translate the stories, and let Japan speak for itself. I hope to welcome you soon.

Tomoya Iwamae — Founder, ARKLinks
FAQ

Questions, answered.

How much does a private journey cost?

+

Journeys are quoted individually, as each is designed from scratch. As a guide, private day experiences begin at ¥80,000 per guest for parties of two or more, including your dedicated guide-interpreter and all arrangements. Fully bespoke multi-day journeys are quoted on request.

Do I need to speak Japanese?

+

Not at all. A licensed bilingual guide-interpreter accompanies every journey, and all communication before and during your trip is in English.

Can we combine several experiences?

+

Yes — most guests do. A tea farm in the morning and a winery in the afternoon, or a multi-day journey across sake country. Tell us your interests and dates, and we will design around them.

Can I buy what I taste and ship it home?

+

Yes. When you fall in love with a maker's work — and you will — we arrange purchases and international shipping directly from the producer, and can continue seasonal deliveries after you return home.

How far in advance should we book?

+

We recommend four weeks or more, as producer visits depend on the season and the makers' schedules — harvests, brewing periods and picking seasons all shape what is possible. Shorter notice is sometimes possible; please ask.

Do you work with hotels and travel designers?

+

Yes. We partner with hotel concierge teams and luxury travel designers to create producer experiences for their guests. Please contact us for partnership details.
